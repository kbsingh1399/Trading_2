3. Methodology: are you moving in the right direction?
Mostly yes, on structure. The Round-2 insight is correct: on a linear ramp, session-VWAP Z tends to √3 ≈ 1.73. So |Z| ≥ 2 picks out accelerating cascades and noise excursions, not exhaustion. Routing on regime first and using Z only as a location measure is the right fix.
The five-pillar stack is not five independent pieces of evidence:
Z, CVD, reconstructed liquidation bands and stop clusters are all functions of the same recent price path. Counting confluences overstates the evidence.
The fifth pillar, walls on another venue, is “sampled receipt recurrence” with no order IDs. That makes it easy to spoof.
Stacking about 20 conjunctive conditions does not create edge. It selects rare events whose base rate you have never measured.
The bigger problem is that the pipeline cannot learn. dg_context sets p_win_lower_bound to None whenever p_win_lower_bound_calibrated is not True. The telemetry publishes calibrated=false (the sample is 17, against a minimum of 100). _net_ev then fails every candidate with EV_p_win_lower_bound_missing.
If features come from the snapshot as published, “0 of 24 admissible” is therefore guaranteed by the code, not a market judgement. No gated trade can ever build the sample that would lift the block. The way out is to log shadow fills (markout_report already exists), not to flip the flag by hand.
The “+330,766 USD, 20/20 OOS” backtest claims in ACTIVE_CONTEXT should be treated as a red flag for lookahead or overfitting until reproduced deterministically. A 10/10 rating from an LLM council is not evidence.
Does the dual-engine split prevent whipsaws and knife-catching? The logic is sound. Three caveats:
Model 1 is not locked to MEAN_REVERT in code. m1_range_override_ok admits it in UNDEFINED regimes using caller-supplied flags (htf_adverse_trend, absorption_verified). That contradicts your stand-aside mandate (P1-1).
The trend vote relies on a slope t-stat on price levels. That statistic is spurious on a random walk (P2-2).
Model 2’s feasible region is very narrow. B6 needs a stop ≥ 1.5 ATR and RR ≥ 2.0, so reward ≥ 3.0 ATR, while the horizon cap is 4.0 ATR. Model 2 can only fire with a stop of 1.5–2.0 ATR, and any stop wider than 2.0 ATR is impossible.
Is standing aside on UNDEFINED correct? Yes, but the reason is expected value, not game theory. Without a validated model for that regime, the sign of conditional drift is unknown, so the expected value of entering is roughly minus friction. You only leave positive EV on the table once a validated UNDEFINED-regime model exists, and none does. The real cost is a tiny opportunity set, which feeds the calibration deadlock.
The LLM council should be allowed to veto but never to authorise. Rebuttals #3 and #4 currently invite narrative arguments to override gates. Rebuttal #4 is invalid on MT5 by your own Round-2 audit: MT5 CFDs have no queue to lose.

Failure modes:
Cold start. With no median yet, b̂ = b_now, so drift is exactly 0 and the breaker can never trip. microstructure_state documents this hazard, but the consumer still falls back silently.
Missing venue mid. If binance_mid is missing, the basis is set to 0 and basis_drift_ok=True. With a negative basis, that understates distance, so walls can be accepted that do not actually sit behind the entry. This is the proximity illusion in its unsafe direction.
Unsynchronised clocks. b_now is a live broker quote minus a snapshot Binance mid that can be about 60 s old. For BTC (ATR about 104), price diffusion over 60 s is roughly 0.2 ATR, almost the whole 0.25 ATR tolerance. The breaker therefore fires on clock skew, not on dislocation. Also, the broker time must be UTC-calibrated: Blueberry’s EET offset is 10,800 s.
One basis for several venues. The Binance basis is applied to Hyperliquid (0x…) walls.
Flash-crash behaviour. If MT5 lags Binance, b_now jumps and the breaker trips, which is correct, but only if the Binance mid is fresh. If the dislocation lasts longer than about 15 minutes (half the window), the median re-anchors and the breaker goes quiet. A long anchor is needed to catch that.
Wrong failure label. A tripped breaker shows up as “no eligible whale wall”. That is exactly the confusion between basis skew and absent depth that Rebuttal 9 wants kept apart.
Should the threshold scale with basis volatility? Yes, with two limits:
Tolerance = max(2 ticks, min(0.25·ATR, 4·σ̂_b)), where σ̂_b = 1.4826·MAD of recent basis prints.
The leftover basis error should be charged against the band: require drift ≤ d ≤ 0.25 − drift (in ATR units). If you translate with an uncertain basis, the uncertainty must come out of the allowed distance.
